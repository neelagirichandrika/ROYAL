TRANSPARENT BY DESIGN MODEL NOTES

Pressure, made measurable.

A transparent state score built from the chase context available in the supplied ball-by-ball data.

METHOD
01.0
01 / PRESSURE STATE

Score the situation before the ball.

For each second-innings delivery, the model reconstructs the score, wickets lost, legal balls remaining, and runs required. It converts three chase signals into a 0–1 index.

CHASE PRESSURE INDEX 0–1 SCALE
0.65 ×required-rate stress+0.20 ×wickets lost+0.15 ×innings progress
RRR stressclamp((required run rate − 6) / 9)
Wickets lostclamp(wickets lost / 6)
Innings progressballs used / 75% of innings length
PRESSURE DELIVERYINDEX ≥ 0.45
i

Only the chase innings are scored because the target is known. This makes the batting and bowling comparison use the same delivery set.

02 / PERFORMANCE VS BASELINE

Compare like with like.

Each player is compared with their own non-pressure chase output inside the selected competition and season scope.

BATTERRuns per 100 balls facedPressure rate − baseline rate
BOWLERRuns per 100 legal ballsBaseline rate − pressure rate
03 / SAMPLE RELIABILITY

Small samples carry less weight.

The raw impact is multiplied by a reliability factor that pulls low-volume scores toward zero.

n / (n + 45)n = pressure balls for this player

04 / ELIGIBILITY

Minimum evidence to rank.

30minimum pressure and baseline balls
3minimum pressure and baseline match counts
Pressure and baseline samples must both clear these minimums.
READ WITH CARE
  • Pressure weights and the 0.45 cut-off are analyst-defined assumptions.
  • First-innings pressure and match win probability are not modeled.
  • Ratings describe observed output, not causal skill or future performance.
  • Confidence intervals use delivery-level variance and do not account for all match clustering.
